What does blood contain? (approximate percentages)

Blood is made of a liquid part called plasma and formed elements (blood cells and platelets). These figures are typical approximate values for whole blood and can vary by person and clinical condition.

🩸 Main parts of whole blood

Plasma
~55%
Formed elements
~45%

Formed elements include red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets. The proportion is commonly described using the hematocrit, which mainly reflects red blood cells.

💧 What plasma contains

Water
~92%
Proteins
~7%
Other solutes
~1%

Other solutes include electrolytes, nutrients, hormones, dissolved gases and waste products. These percentages are rounded.

🔴 Formed elements (approximate volume)

Red cells
~44%
White cells + platelets
<1%

Red cells make up nearly all of the formed-element volume. White blood cells and platelets together form a very small fraction, often called the buffy coat.

🧬 Main jobs of blood

Red blood cells: carry oxygen using haemoglobin.

White blood cells: help defend against infection.

Platelets: help form clots to control bleeding.

Plasma: transports proteins, nutrients, hormones and waste.

Important: These percentages describe the approximate composition of normal whole blood, not the percentage of each blood group available in a blood bank. Blood-bank stock is counted by tested units and components, not by composition percentages. Clinical decisions must be made by qualified healthcare staff.

Blood information and components

A simple guide to what a blood bank may collect, test, store and issue under clinical supervision.

🩸 Whole blood

Contains red cells, plasma, platelets and other components. Use depends on clinical need and local protocols.

🔴 Packed red blood cells (PRBC)

Primarily used to improve the blood’s oxygen-carrying capacity when a clinician determines a transfusion is needed.

🟡 Platelets

Help blood clot. Platelet products have special storage requirements and often a shorter shelf life than red-cell products.

💛 Plasma

The liquid part of blood containing clotting proteins. Plasma use and compatibility depend on the clinical situation.

🧬 Cryoprecipitate

A plasma-derived component rich in selected clotting proteins, issued for specific medical indications.

🧪 Testing and matching

Blood banks follow donor screening, infectious-disease testing, blood grouping and compatibility procedures before issue.

Blood group alone is not enough to decide whether a unit is suitable. The treating clinical team and blood bank must confirm compatibility and patient-specific requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this website to confirm live blood stock?

Not yet. This version displays clearly labelled example inventory. Live counts require a verified data connection to participating blood banks.

Does the same blood group always mean a match?

No. The hospital and blood bank must perform the appropriate testing and compatibility checks for the patient.

Can I request a donor directly?

The prototype has no real registered donors. A production service needs opt-in donor registration, consent, privacy safeguards and eligibility screening.

What should I do in an emergency?

Contact the treating hospital immediately and ask its team to coordinate with a licensed blood bank. Do not delay treatment while searching online.

Can I use the website offline?

The app shell may open offline after it has been loaded and cached. Live stock, map search, calls and location-based search need connectivity.

How it works

1. Search

Choose the blood group and enter an area, PIN code, or allow location access.

2. Contact and confirm

Call the centre or hospital to verify stock, compatibility, eligibility, and collection / delivery instructions.

3. Coordinate safely

Hospitals and licensed blood banks should manage testing, matching, and transfusion decisions.

Important for a real deployment

To turn this prototype into a trustworthy public service, connect it to verified licensed blood-bank data and hospital workflows. Do not publish personal donor phone numbers or promise stock without live confirmation.

  • Partner with licensed blood banks / hospitals for real-time stock updates.
  • Use verified maps and distance calculations, with a manual city/PIN fallback.
  • Add donor consent, eligibility screening, privacy protections, and abuse reporting.
  • Use professional translations and accessible text/audio support for all listed languages.
  • Keep emergency instructions clear; never replace clinicians or blood-bank screening.
